Dear Anna,

Following our call last Thursday, I am writing to formalise the key discussion points for the 2025 supply framework between EuroCharge Assembly (Budapest) and Asian Injection Molding (Ho Chi Minh City) for injection-moulded plastic components (EVC programme).

CONTEXT
Our current annual volume is 2,500 EV charging station units, of which each unit requires 8.2 kg of injection-moulded ABS plastic parts. For 2025, our production plan calls for 3,200 units (+28% YoY), driven by new customer contracts in the Nordics and Benelux.

OUR POSITION ON PRICING
Current agreed price: EUR 2.12/kg (2024 framework)
Your proposal for 2025: EUR 2.28/kg (+7.5%)
Our counter-proposal: EUR 2.18/kg (+2.8%), conditional on a 12-month volume commitment of 26,240 kg (3,200 units × 8.2 kg)

We believe EUR 2.18/kg is fair given the volume uplift we are committing to, which de-risks your production planning. We are also prepared to offer a 15% upfront order (approx. 4,000 kg) in Q1 2025 to support your material procurement.

QUALITY REQUIREMENTS FOR 2025
As discussed, we will be introducing stricter incoming inspection criteria from January 2025 in response to a quality event in 2024 (connector-related, separate to your parts). Specifically:
  – First article inspection (FAI) required for any new mould tool or mould modification
  – CoA required per batch (min. tensile strength, Izod impact, MFI)
  – Maximum sea freight transit time of 30 days (current 28 days is acceptable; we need buffer commitment)

DUAL-SOURCING NOTE
I want to be transparent: we are currently qualifying a European regional supplier for plastic components as a risk mitigation measure (Risk R1 in our supply risk register). This is not intended to reduce our commitment to AIM but to protect our supply chain against long lead-time disruptions. We anticipate the European source would cover no more than 15% of annual volume.

Next steps: Could you please come back to us with a revised proposal by 20 September? I would like to bring a signed heads-of-agreement to our management review on 30 September.
